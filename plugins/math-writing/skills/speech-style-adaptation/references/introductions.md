# Introductions and thesis structure

Use this reference when drafting or substantially restructuring an
introduction. For a local voice pass, preserve the existing architecture.

## Article introduction

The calibration paper uses this progression:

1. orient the reader with the relevant classical fact;
2. state the limitation or open gap;
3. say what the paper does;
4. give the standing setup and key definitions;
5. connect the new result to the closest prior result;
6. sketch the construction when it helps the reader understand the theorem;
7. state the main results;
8. describe the paper's contents by section;
9. place acknowledgments last when the venue uses that convention.

This is a useful pattern, not a mandatory nine-part template. Omit stages that
do not serve the paper, and do not invent novelty, literature claims, or
motivation to fill a slot.

## Thesis hierarchy

Keep broad background in the thesis introduction. Part introductions should
frame their common theme; chapter introductions should give local motivation
and results without repeating the global background. Discover the actual
document hierarchy instead of assuming filenames or directories.

For a chapter derived from joint work, retain the mathematical first-person
plural unless the author asks otherwise. Add or change a contribution statement
only from author-supplied facts. Do not infer individual contributions from the
paper or its author list.

Funding, affiliation, address, email, acknowledgments, and referee language are
factual metadata, not voice calibration. Preserve existing text or request the
current facts; never copy these details from `jems4.tex`.
